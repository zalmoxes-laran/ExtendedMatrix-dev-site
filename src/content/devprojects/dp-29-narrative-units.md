desc: "First-class formalisation of narrative events (dialogues, character interactions, ritual sequences, performed actions) as stratigraphic objects in the EM graph, anchored to a real-time playback axis that runs horizontally alongside the existing vertical chronological matrix. Prototyped in Paola Frigerio's master thesis (Padova 2022) as Extended Matrix Narratives — a working-journal methodology for collaboration between creative industries and academia toward edutainment projects, validated through a case study built around three Plautine plays. DP-29 carries the thesis prototype into the EM language proper: NarrativeUnit nodes, Character system, real-time playback axis distinct from past-state vertical time, integration with Heriverse for in-browser narrative playback."
components:
  - "**NarrativeUnit node class** in s3dgraphy datamodel — a stratigraphic-class sibling of SF / VSF / US that represents a discrete narrative event (a line of dialogue, a gesture, a movement across the scene, a ritual act). Carries: `actors` (one or more Character references), `register` (linguistic / behavioural register, e.g. ‘colloquial Latin Plautine register’), `duration` (real-time playback length), `prereq` (what must have happened in the narrative before this unit can fire), `consequence` (what state of the scene this unit produces). Dual-typed in CIDOC: `crminf:I1_Argumentation` (the modeller's narrative claim, with its sources and paradata) plus `crm:E5_Event` (the in-narrative event itself). Keeps the epistemic provenance of the narrative claim separate from the in-fiction event it asserts."
  - "**Character node class** — a stratigraphic-class sibling for the in-narrative actors (characters in the dramatic / ritual sense, not the Unicode sense). Each Character carries an identity (name, gender, social role) and a behavioural register profile that NarrativeUnits reference. Sub-class of `crmsoc:Actor` (the social-relations CIDOC extension) so the in-narrative agents inherit the CIDOC actor surface — relationships, group memberships, role assignments — without inheriting historicity (a Character is a *reconstructed* actor, never asserted as a historical person)."
  - "**Real-time playback axis (horizontal time)** — a second temporal dimension on the EM canvas, orthogonal to the existing vertical chronological matrix. The vertical axis carries *past chronology* (which year, which epoch, which phase); the horizontal axis carries *real-time narrative duration* (minute 3 of the dialogue, second 12 of the ritual). A NarrativeUnit lives at coordinates (chronology, playback_offset) — it both belongs to a past epoch AND occupies a specific moment in the narrative's real-time unfolding. Visualisation in yEd: the existing swimlanes-by-epoch convention extended with a perpendicular ‘time-rail’ that shows the playback ordering inside each epoch."
  - "**Edges between NarrativeUnits**: `precedes_narrative` (strict ordering in playback), `concurrent_with` (two units overlap in playback time), `triggers` (one unit's consequence enables another's prereq). These are real-time-axis edges, distinct from the chronological `is_before` / `is_after` edges on the vertical axis. CIDOC mapping: `crm:P120_occurs_before` for `precedes_narrative`, `crm:P116_starts` / `crm:P117_occurs_during` for `concurrent_with`, custom `em:` extension for `triggers`."
  - "**Edges from NarrativeUnits to stratigraphic / virtual units**: `takes_place_in` (a NarrativeUnit happens in a USV that represents a past space — atrium, threshold, portico), `involves_artifact` (a NarrativeUnit references an SF / VSF — e.g. a character picks up a cup). These close the loop between the past-state reconstruction (vertical) and the narrative playback (horizontal): the dialogue happens IN the reconstructed atrium and WITH the reconstructed cup."
  - "**EM Tools authoring affordance** — a Narrative Units panel parallel to the Activity / Epoch / TimeBranch managers (DP-12 + DP-43). Horizontal timeline view with NarrativeUnits as blocks ordered by `precedes_narrative` edges; characters appear as horizontal lanes (one swimlane per character); the modeller drags, reorders, groups, edits register / duration / prereq inline; a register picker reads from the Vocabulary Project (DP-09)."
  - "**Heriverse playback** (DP-11 integration) — when a published EM model is opened in Heriverse with a NarrativeUnit graph attached, the viewer offers a *play* affordance: the narrative unfolds in real-time on top of the 3D scene, characters animate or display subtitled dialogue, the visitor scrubs the playback or jumps to a specific NarrativeUnit. The reconstruction stops being a static frozen moment of the past and becomes an inhabited one. A one-click ‘show sources’ surface lists the documentary attestations behind each line for visitors who want the paradata."
  - "**Working-journal methodology** (the Frigerio thesis contribution) — NarrativeUnits are not only a runtime construct but a **collaborative authoring methodology** for projects that bridge creative industries (writers, designers, performers) and academia (archaeologists, historians, philologists). Each NarrativeUnit's `register` field is validated against documented historical sources — in the Frigerio case study, three Plautine plays — so the dialogue is not invented freely but anchored to a documentary tradition. The EM graph then serves as the shared notebook between the two communities: the writer proposes a dialogue, the academic validates its register against attestable models, and the assertions of both parties live in the same graph as queryable claims with full paradata."
  - "Validation rule: a NarrativeUnit must be linked to at least one Character (the actor) and to at least one chronological epoch (the past time it happens in). Validation at parse time + on every graph mutation. Helpful error message when violated."
  - "Round-trip discipline: NarrativeUnit and Character nodes round-trip through the canonical-edges + multilingual-rapporti pipeline (DP-62 + DP-63 PR #22/#23) the same way every other stratigraphic-class node does. pyArchInit has no native concept of narrative units; the reverse export to a pyArchInit DB drops them with a warning (or stores them in a side-channel column when a real consumer needs round-trip fidelity)."
  - "RDF / SPARQL queries enabled by the new classes — ‘list all NarrativeUnits in a given epoch ordered by playback time’, ‘list characters by behavioural register across the project’, ‘trace which Plautine plays were used as register sources for which dialogues’, ‘count NarrativeUnits per Character to find the protagonists of the reconstructive narrative’. Native to the v1.6 RDF Export layer once the new classes are declared in `em.ttl`."

Extended Matrix has been documenting the **past** for over a decade — the past as a sequence of stratigraphic events, the past as a configuration of physical and virtual units, the past as a network of reconstructive claims supported by evidence. What it has not formalised, until DP-29 picks up Paola Frigerio's master-thesis prototype, is the **performance** of that past in the present: a dialogue spoken in a reconstructed atrium, a ritual gesture re-enacted at a temple threshold, a market exchange staged in a portico — events that happened then, are unfolding now in real-time, and that an edutainment workflow needs to express as first-class assertions in the same graph that documents the rest of the reconstruction.

The Frigerio thesis (Università di Padova, 2022) named the prototype **Extended Matrix Narratives** and identified its primary use case: bridging the methodological gap between **creative industries** (writers, performers, designers — the people who produce the dialogues, gestures, and scenes that bring a reconstruction to life) and **academia** (archaeologists, historians, philologists — the people whose validation grounds those dialogues and gestures in the documentary record). The thesis showed how a tool that lets both communities live in the same graph — the writer authoring a dialogue, the academic validating its register against documented sources — turns the EM graph into a **working journal** for edutainment projects that go beyond entertainment without becoming dry dissemination. The case study used three Plautine plays as the register source for a sample Latin dialogue; the result was theoretical, prototyped, and waiting for the EM language to formalise it.

DP-29 closes that gap. The thesis prototype becomes a permanent language extension: **NarrativeUnit** as a stratigraphic-class sibling of SF, VSF and US, with its own paradata chain, its own roundtrip discipline, its own SPARQL surface; **Character** as a sub-class of `crmsoc:Actor` for the in-narrative agents who carry the dialogues and gestures; a **real-time playback axis** (horizontal time) that runs orthogonal to the existing vertical chronological matrix, so a NarrativeUnit is both anchored to a past epoch AND positioned at a specific moment in the playback timeline; **edges between NarrativeUnits** for ordering and concurrency (`precedes_narrative`, `concurrent_with`, `triggers`); **edges from NarrativeUnits to the rest of the graph** for the spatial-physical anchor (`takes_place_in`, `involves_artifact`) so the dialogue happens IN the reconstructed atrium and WITH the reconstructed cup.

**Why the horizontal axis matters.** The existing EM time dimension is vertical for a reason — it expresses chronology: the depth of time, the layering of past upon past, the Harris-style ordering of one phase atop another. But chronology and **playback** are different temporal phenomena. A dialogue between two characters in a Roman atrium has a chronological position (it happens in 130 BCE, in the Augustan refurbishment phase, in the *atrium* room) and a playback position (line 1 is at second 0, line 2 at second 4, line 3 at second 9). The two coordinates are orthogonal: the dialogue happens in the same chronological moment whether the playback runs at normal speed, double speed, or paused. Conflating them onto a single axis would make either the chronology unreadable (if the playback dominates) or the playback inexpressible (if the chronology dominates). DP-29's horizontal axis is therefore not a cosmetic choice — it is a structural one that lets the EM graph carry both.

**The working-journal methodology, in concrete sequence.** A reconstruction team at a Roman site commissions an edutainment piece — a five-minute dialogue between three characters in the atrium of a senatorial house, set in 130 BCE. The writer proposes the dialogue as a sequence of NarrativeUnits in the EM graph, each specifying its actor (Character), its line of speech (free text), and its claimed register (a controlled-vocabulary entry, e.g. ‘Latin colloquial — Plautine register’). The academic reviewer opens the same graph, queries the SPARQL endpoint for *‘all NarrativeUnits with register Plautine, plus the documentary sources that validate the register’*, and discovers that one of the lines references a colloquialism actually attested in Terence rather than Plautus. They propose a register change to ‘Latin colloquial — Terentian register’ and document the source citation as a complex source via DP-66's `is_premise_of` edge. The writer accepts (or pushes back), and the graph carries both versions as alternative TimeBranches (DP-12). When the piece eventually ships in Heriverse (DP-11), the visitor sees the actors speaking lines that have been validated, and a one-click *show sources* surface lists the documentary attestations behind each line. The reconstruction is no longer just *what the building looked like* — it is *who said what to whom in the building, on what evidence*.

**Why this is in the EM 2.0 horizon rather than 1.7.** The work is a substantial language extension — two new stratigraphic-class siblings, a new temporal axis, six new edge types, integration with Heriverse playback, controlled-vocabulary integration via DP-09 — and benefits from sitting on top of several pieces shipping in 1.6 / 1.7: DP-66's property inheritance (Character profiles inheriting from cultural patterns via `was_derived_from`), DP-12's TimeBranch alternatives (narrative branches as alternative readings), DP-63's multilingual labels (the `register` vocabulary across languages), DP-32's propagative resolver (author / license / embargo on NarrativeUnits). The thesis prototype was already theoretically complete in 2022; what DP-29 needs is the surrounding EM language infrastructure to mature first, which the 1.6 / 1.7 cycle is delivering. Once that lands, DP-29 is a self-contained extension with clear cross-references and a Frigerio-validated case study, ready to ship as a 2.0 cornerstone — Extended Matrix moves from documenting the past to **performing it**.

**Open decisions for the review call** (six points): (1) NarrativeUnit as new class vs sub-type of Activity (proposed: new class — Activity is too coarse); (2) Character as `em:Character` subclassing `crmsoc:Actor` (proposed: yes); (3) horizontal time axis as structural field + visualisation wrapper vs visualisation-only (proposed: structural — data carries playback semantics independent of yEd); (4) `register` as controlled vocabulary via DP-09 (proposed: yes, with multilingual extension via DP-63); (5) Heriverse playback affordance — subtitle overlay vs text-to-speech (proposed: subtitle MVP, TTS as 2.x enhancement); (6) the thesis prototype was Plautine Latin — DP-29 generalises to any historical register, and the Vocabulary Project (DP-09) is the right home for the cross-project register catalogue.
